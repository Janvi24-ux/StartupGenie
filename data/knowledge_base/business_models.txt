BUSINESS MODELS AND MARKET RESEARCH – KNOWLEDGE BASE
======================================================

## Business Model Canvas (9 Building Blocks)

### 1. Customer Segments
- Who are you creating value for?
- Mass Market, Niche Market, Segmented, Diversified, Multi-sided Platform.
- B2B (Business-to-Business): Target companies; longer sales cycles, higher deal values.
- B2C (Business-to-Consumer): Target individuals; shorter cycles, higher volume, brand-driven.
- B2B2C: Sell to businesses who serve consumers (e.g., SaaS for retailers).
- D2C (Direct-to-Consumer): Cut intermediaries; higher margin, direct relationship.

### 2. Value Propositions
- What value do you deliver to the customer?
- Newness, Performance, Customisation, "Getting the Job Done", Design, Brand/Status.
- Price, Cost Reduction, Risk Reduction, Accessibility, Convenience/Usability.

### 3. Channels
- How do you reach customers?
- Awareness, Evaluation, Purchase, Delivery, After Sales.
- Direct: Own website, sales team, physical stores.
- Indirect: Partners, distributors, resellers, marketplaces (Amazon, Flipkart).

### 4. Customer Relationships
- What type of relationship does each segment expect?
- Personal Assistance, Dedicated Personal Assistance, Self-Service, Automated Services.
- Communities, Co-creation.

### 5. Revenue Streams
- For what value are customers willing to pay?
- Asset Sale, Usage Fee, Subscription, Lending/Renting/Leasing.
- Licensing, Brokerage Fee, Advertising.

### 6. Key Resources
- Physical, Intellectual, Human, Financial.

### 7. Key Activities
- Production, Problem Solving, Platform/Network.

### 8. Key Partnerships
- Buyers-Suppliers, Joint Ventures, Strategic Alliances, Co-opetition.
- Reasons: Optimisation and economy of scale, Reduction of risk, Acquisition of resources.

### 9. Cost Structure
- Cost-Driven vs Value-Driven.
- Fixed Costs: Rent, salaries, server costs.
- Variable Costs: Raw materials, commissions.
- Economies of Scale, Economies of Scope.

## Revenue Model Patterns

### SaaS (Software as a Service)
- Monthly/Annual Recurring Revenue (MRR/ARR).
- Tiered pricing: Freemium → Basic → Pro → Enterprise.
- Key metrics: MRR, ARR, Churn Rate, Customer Lifetime Value (LTV), Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), LTV/CAC ratio (target 3:1).

### Marketplace / Platform
- Take-rate (commission) on transactions; typically 5–30%.
- Examples: Swiggy (food delivery), Urban Company (home services).
- Key metrics: GMV, Take-rate, Number of active buyers, Number of active sellers.

### E-Commerce / D2C
- Margin on products sold.
- Key metrics: Revenue per order, Return rate, Repeat purchase rate, Average Order Value (AOV).

### Subscription Box
- Fixed monthly/quarterly fee.
- Key metrics: Subscriber count, Churn, Box margin.

### Freemium
- Core product free; charge for premium features.
- Examples: Spotify, Dropbox, Notion.
- Key metrics: Free-to-paid conversion rate (industry average: 2–5%).

### Licensing
- License technology/IP to third parties.
- Upfront license fee + ongoing royalties.

### Advertising
- Free product/service; revenue from advertisers.
- Key metrics: Monthly Active Users (MAU), Daily Active Users (DAU), CPM, CPC.

### Asset-Heavy / CapEx Model
- Requires significant physical assets.
- Examples: Manufacturing, logistics, real estate.

## Market Sizing Framework

### TAM (Total Addressable Market)
- The total revenue opportunity if you captured 100% of the market.
- Calculate: (Number of potential customers) × (Annual revenue per customer).

### SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market)
- Portion of TAM targeted by your product/service given geography, segment focus.

### SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market)
- Realistic market share you can capture in the short term (1–3 years).
- Typically 1–5% of SAM for an early-stage startup.

## Competitive Analysis Frameworks

### Porter's Five Forces
1. Threat of new entrants (barriers to entry).
2. Bargaining power of suppliers.
3. Bargaining power of buyers.
4. Threat of substitute products.
5. Competitive rivalry within the industry.

### SWOT Analysis
- Strengths (internal), Weaknesses (internal), Opportunities (external), Threats (external).

### Competitive Positioning Matrix
- Plot competitors on axes (e.g., Price vs Quality, Features vs Simplicity).
- Identify whitespace (underserved segment).

## Unit Economics

### Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
- Total Sales & Marketing spend / Number of new customers acquired.
- Benchmark: Depends on industry; for SaaS, target CAC payback < 12 months.

### Customer Lifetime Value (LTV or CLV)
- LTV = (Average revenue per user × Gross margin) / Churn rate.
- Target LTV:CAC ratio of at least 3:1.

### Burn Rate
- Monthly cash outflow from operations.
- Runway = Cash in bank / Monthly burn rate.
- Target runway: 18–24 months post-funding.

### Gross Margin
- (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue × 100%.
- SaaS: 70–90%; E-commerce: 30–50%; Marketplace: 60–80%.

## Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy

### Inbound Marketing
- Content marketing, SEO, social media.
- Lower CAC; takes 6–18 months to build momentum.

### Outbound Marketing
- Cold email, cold calling, paid ads (Google, Meta, LinkedIn).
- Faster results; higher CAC.

### Product-Led Growth (PLG)
- Product itself drives user acquisition (freemium, viral loops).
- Examples: Zoom, Slack, Figma.
- Key metric: Virality coefficient (k-factor > 1 means viral growth).

### Partnership-Led Growth
- Channel partners, OEMs, resellers.
- Suitable for enterprise B2B.

### Community-Led Growth
- Build a community around the product.
- Lower CAC; high retention; brand moat.
