STARTUP FUNDING – COMPREHENSIVE KNOWLEDGE BASE
================================================

## Startup Funding Stages

### Pre-Seed Stage
- Definition: The earliest stage of funding where founders use personal savings, family, and friends to validate an idea.
- Typical Amount: INR 5 lakh – INR 50 lakh (USD 5,000 – USD 100,000)
- Purpose: Idea validation, market research, building a prototype.
- Sources: Personal savings (Bootstrapping), Friends & Family, Angel investors, Incubators.

### Seed Stage
- Definition: First formal round of funding to scale operations after product-market fit is found.
- Typical Amount: INR 50 lakh – INR 5 crore (USD 100,000 – USD 2 million)
- Purpose: Hiring initial team, product development, initial marketing.
- Sources: Angel investors, Seed VCs, Accelerators (Y Combinator, Techstars), Government seed funds.

### Series A
- Definition: First significant round of institutional VC funding.
- Typical Amount: INR 5 crore – INR 50 crore (USD 2 million – USD 15 million)
- Purpose: Proven business model, scaling operations, entering new markets.
- Sources: Venture Capital firms, Corporate VCs.

### Series B
- Definition: Scaling a proven business model further.
- Typical Amount: INR 50 crore – INR 200 crore (USD 15 million – USD 50 million)
- Purpose: Market expansion, building team, scaling technology.
- Sources: Larger VC firms, Private Equity.

### Series C and Beyond
- Definition: Late-stage funding for mature startups looking to scale significantly or prepare for IPO.
- Typical Amount: INR 200 crore+ (USD 50 million+)
- Sources: Private Equity, Hedge Funds, Investment Banks.

### IPO (Initial Public Offering)
- Definition: Startup lists on a stock exchange (NSE/BSE in India) to raise capital from public.

## Types of Investors

### Angel Investors
- High-net-worth individuals investing personal capital in early-stage startups.
- Typical investment: INR 25 lakh – INR 2 crore.
- Networks: Indian Angel Network (IAN), Mumbai Angels, LetsVenture, AngelList India.
- Expect equity stake of 5–20% at pre-seed/seed stage.

## Funding Instruments

### Equity
- Common Stock: Founders hold this; dilutes with every round.
- Preferred Stock: Investor-held; has liquidation preference and anti-dilution protections.

### Convertible Instruments
- Convertible Notes: Debt that converts to equity at next round, often with a discount (typically 20%) and a valuation cap.
- SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity): Common in US; gaining traction in India.
- Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS): Most common in India for VC deals.

### Debt Financing
- Revenue-Based Financing (RBF): Repay from monthly revenue, typically 1–3x capital.
- Venture Debt: Debt funding for VC-backed startups; Trifecta Capital, Innoven Capital, Alteria Capital are key players.

## Valuation Methods for Startups
- Berkus Method: Assigns value to 5 key success factors (max $500K each in early stage).
- Scorecard Valuation: Compares startup to funded peers on multiple dimensions.
- Discounted Cash Flow (DCF): Projects future cash flows and discounts to present value (more applicable for later-stage).
- Revenue Multiple: Typical SaaS multiples range from 5x–15x ARR at Series A.
- Market Multiple: Valuation based on comparable company transactions.

## Key Terms in Term Sheets
- Valuation Cap: Maximum valuation at which notes convert to equity.
- Discount Rate: Discount on next round price for convertible note holders (typically 15–25%).
- Liquidation Preference: Investor gets back investment before founders in an exit.
- Pro-rata Rights: Right to invest in future rounds to maintain ownership percentage.
- Anti-dilution Provisions: Protects investors from valuation drops in subsequent rounds.
- Vesting Schedule: Typical 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff for founders and employees.

## Pitch Deck Essentials
1. Problem – What pain point are you solving?
2. Solution – How do you solve it uniquely?
3. Market Size – TAM, SAM, SOM analysis.
4. Product – Demo, screenshots, key features.
5. Business Model – How you make money.
6. Traction – Revenue, users, growth metrics.
7. Team – Why this team can execute.
8. Financials – 3-year projections.
9. Ask – How much are you raising and how will you use it?
